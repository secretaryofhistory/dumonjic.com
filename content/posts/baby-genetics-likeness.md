In my hands lay identical twins 30 years apart.

In my right hand, our two-week old daughter rests, her pinkish eyelids with two faint creases covering her eyes, split by her faux bridge that slides to a shiny triangular nose with softened corners, completed by lips shaped like an underlined capital “M”.

In my left hand, a headshot, like the bust of Nefertiti, of the original creation from 30 years earlier: my wife, the most beautiful woman in my eyes. Nature must share the sentiment because it looked at my wife’s facial genetics and determined improvements could not be made, and passed them on to our daughter. Thankfully, it avoided pooling my middling genes and creating a [combined look](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Polygenic-Trait).

As I stare at the two, it’s a mystery to me how nature decides to divvy genes across the human body. It’s hard to believe that it’s random because nature seems to have *some* intentionality. Decisions must be based on *something*; even a haphazardly planted flower grows in the direction of the sunlight. Maybe decisions are made based on the environment in utero similar to [epigenetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetics) where the genes are shaped by the environment? Perhaps they’re influenced by external factors during pregnancy like nutrition or stress? What about some genes simply cancelling others out?

In the appearance of our daughter, nature decided to section off likeness based on different parts of the body. Though her face is like my wife’s, her arms and torso and legs are long like mine, including her fingers and toes. It’s like they were stretched across the three decades (34 years; I rounded down) between her birth and mine. Their length has already caused the same problems for her, such as where to put them when sleeping. I try to fold them to make them shorter, whereas she extends them straight into the air like asking someone to free her of them.

The mix of traits has also left me wondering in the quiet hours what she will look like in the future. I can imagine her 5-foot-7 with long, thin arms and legs, brown hair and brown eyes. Her hair is currently blond, as are her eyebrows and extra-long eyelashes, though that may change based on other genes in our families, namely both of our fathers who had blond hair at birth before browning later. Her eye color has not yet come in, so it could be brown like mine or blue-green like my wife’s, but this is merely a guess. All of this is a guess.

Maybe nature is guessing too and random, after all.
