You and I have taken for granted the act of learning something new. When we learn something new as an adult, we acknowledge it and move onto the next act or task immediately. It’s one after another without stopping, without appreciating the lessons we’ve learned.

When was the last time you learned something new and stopped to think about it thoroughly, as if you were explaining it to yourself again or simply appreciating the knowledge gained?

I’ve been asking myself this recently because I’ve been learning a lot as a dad and a salaryman but haven’t stopped to not only acknowledge it, but repeat the lesson over and over again like our daughter does.

In the last couple of months, our daughter has learned how to smile, to track things in the air with her eyes, converse (unintelligibly), hold things, roll onto her belly, lift her head, sleep independently, hang out on her own, and so much more. I saw these life lessons happen in real-time, saw her eyes open long and wide, saw her repeat her learnings day after day. I’m so proud of her (and she is too; just clap) and along the way have realized that I have forgotten to be sincerely proud of myself.

I have never taken the time to be proud of any accomplishments. Part of it is because of the mental preparation that I’ve gone through to achieve the accomplishments, such as visualizing them start to finish before starting, which results in an anti-climactic conclusion. Another part of it is that daily accomplishments feel meaningless because everyone is having them. Why celebrate something that is a regular occurrence throughout the world?

I have been reminded by three individuals (my manager, my work mentor, and my friend) that it should be celebrated. It’s a hard adjustment and one that I’m not sure how to do. Maybe I’ll turn to our daughter for advice, as her method seems to work: open eyes wide, bring hands together, and smile!
