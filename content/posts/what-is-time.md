It was not long ago that time felt real. Time used to be available throughout the day, allowing my wife and I to complete tasks as we pleased, whether planning an outing, having a meal, or checking off our to-do list.

With a baby, time really feels like an idea, as if to say “Wouldn’t it be nice if there was a thing that allowed us to do this?”. The person or people who centuries ago created the 60 minutes in an hour, the 24-hour clock in a day, the 365 days in a year, clearly did not have kids. If they had kids, they would not have organized time in that way. They actually would not have had the time to organize time at all because they would have blinked into bedtime.

My wife and I, alongside our 3-month-old, have realized that some version of truncated time is true and real. We still plan to do things daily like we always have, except we no longer are able to do those things in the same way.

For example, we used to eat dinner at the same time. We would sit at the table (or the couch) and have a meal together and chat or watch TV (when tired). Now the TV is on in the background, one of us is eating, the other is babysitting, and everyone is hoping that there’s not a blowout in the meantime because that will prevent the other from eating immediately (for more than one reason).

In an extreme example, during the workday I’ll be stuck staring at my computer for hours, while my wife is unable to take a break from our daughter, who is constantly cuddling, feeding, or whatever else you can think of that requires my wife to be physically close to her at all times. Sometimes 1 PM arrives, and my wife has not eaten and has drained her beloved Stanley bottle.

These patterns appear again and again in various ways, including to-do lists that take far longer to check off than usual, home projects that are *rightthere* and far from completion at the same time, and plans that discretely dissipate into obscurity.

The puzzling part is that there *are* pockets of time that sometimes develop with potential, but they never quite feel right. They’re hurried through, rushed around, rested about, or simply impossible to realize because they could wake the baby, who is the princess of time and has no concept of time at all.

Ah, time. What is it again? Is it real? How does one find and use it? Did anyone think of its construction with kids? Anyone?
