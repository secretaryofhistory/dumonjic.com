Recently, we bought a baby-wrap carrier as an experiment with a hands-free life.

The carrier is burnt orange and one of those cheap(er) cloth ones that requires a master’s degree to loop around your upper body, so you can look like [Fred Flintstone](https://images-na.ssl-images-amazon.com/images/S/pv-target-images/3cc1b81926533356d27ceda338b0d3dc17c5c309e8d07277d73a22035a4b8530._RI_V_TTW_.jpg).

The idea is that if you have tired arms (raises hand) and a baby who wants to be always held (raises hand slower), you can stuff the baby into this wrap, giving them skin-to-skin contact and freeing up your hands at the same time. Sounds great, right?

It’s a trap. You spend 20 minutes wrapping yourself, starting around your waist, flipping it over your shoulder, looping under, looping over, looping wherever else your mental gymnastics guesses until there’s little cloth left, and then finally insert the baby. And when you insert the baby, you have so many other checks and worries:

Is the baby sitting properly and their legs dangling in an M shape?

Are the arms out and extended, as if the baby is giving you a hug?

Is the baby’s head in the right position, not too far up but not too far down?

Can the baby breathe? Can they?

Once you pass all of the checks, you hope that your baby sleeps for 20 minutes.

If they do sleep and don’t mind noise, the world seems to open up. You start thinking about all of the things you can do, but the trap with this is that you can’t *really* do them.

For example, my wife placed our daughter in the wrap one day and decided to vacuum. She set up the vacuum, turned it on, and pushed it across the second floor of our home. This worked well at first because our house was becoming cleaner, but soon she realized that nothing could be moved out of the way because she had sleeping beauty strapped to her chest and couldn’t really bend over. If she bent over to vacuum underneath the bed, would the baby wake up, or worse? It was all far too risky.

In another example, I wrapped myself, which was like placing a ribbon around the Empire State Building, and stuffed our daughter into the wrap. I started leading the hands-free life, feeling empowered to conquer the world, before realizing that I no longer knew what to do with my hands. If they weren’t holding our daughter as they have constantly for the past two months, what was their use?

In that moment, I decided that my hands were only useful for holding our daughter, so I asked my wife to help me take off the Flintstones outfit. Then I held our daughter until my arms were detaching from my body, and it felt right.
