Our morning routine developed when our daughter started waking up early at the time of the fourth-month regression.

The regression is a transitional period when everything you know about your child is suddenly wrong, and you wonder if this is the same baby that’s been alongside you for the first four months.

She started suddenly waking up around 5 AM. She would feed all night (every 1.5-2 hours), keeping her mother up, and then wake up with the energy of a Red Bull. Because that’s not normal, my wife and I agreed that I should take our daughter downstairs to hang out so my wife can catch up on some sleep.

Downstairs, our routine started to quickly take shape and remains in place today. I started with a diaper change (a must!) and then what I’ve been calling “unstructured playtime” commenced for ~40 minutes.

The playtime consisted of rotating toys placed in a circle around our daughter, who lied down and progressively reached for each toy that interested her. She’d spend a lot of time on her belly, reaching for each toy and bear-crawling toward them one by one. During this time, I didn’t engage with our daughter (unless she looked at me) because it’s important she has a wake-up period and independent playtime. The idea is she doesn’t need me to show and hand her every toy; rather she explores freely and discovers her morning interests, while I sit there with only a cup of coffee and silently observe.

Over the last two months, this unstructured playtime has not changed, but what our daughter does during this time has changed. She no longer lies only on her belly; she now sits up, she reverse-crawls, and she’s on the verge of crawling forward.

When she became tired of this playtime, I moved her to an engaged period in her [activity center](https://www.amazon.com/clp/B01J94K9OY) for ~25 minutes and then I sat on the floor next to her, so we’re eye-level. During this time, she played with the toys attached to the center, which includes a piano that she loved to slap on and watched me to dance.

Eventually, her energy waned, and I picked up a Bosnian children’s story to read (“Crvenkapa” for the win!), which she really enjoys.

Soon after, her eyebrows became pink, her eyes were rubbed by her tiny fists, and I started carrying her around our house to put her to sleep. We spent most of the time at the slider, rocking in place and watching the birds fly by and spotting the occasional outdoor cat. Fifteen minutes later, she was asleep and I took her back to the couch, where she continued sleeping in my arms while I looked for something to watch on the television in 40 minutes because that’s precisely how long she will sleep before the routine restarts.
