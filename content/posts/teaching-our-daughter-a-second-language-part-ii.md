In the two months since our daughter’s birth, we’ve been talking to her in [two languages](/2026/03/08/teaching-child-second-language/). My wife speaks to her in English, I speak to her in Bosnian, and our daughter seemingly acknowledges both.

While she doesn’t necessarily understand what we’re saying, the repetition of words from our individual vocabularies seems like it will help her learn the languages faster. We see some proof of this recently with the words, *tongue* and *jezik (yeh-zeek)*.

Both words mean the same thing, and it’s one of the first words that my wife introduced to our daughter when trying to make her laugh early on. My wife would hold her, say “tongue,” and stick her tongue out, which made our daughter smile. The smile was infectious, so my wife kept repeating it all, and over time our daughter started sticking her tongue out at the same time or immediately after.

As an experiment in continuous and compound learning, I repeated the same process but introduced the word *jezik* to our daughter. I’d hold her, say “*jezik*,” and stick my tongue out, which confused our daughter at first. She’d look at me blankly as if to say “That’s not the word!”.

After more repetitions, particularly during diaper changes to keep her happy, our daughter picked up on it and stuck her tongue out too at the sound of the word in Bosnian.

Sometimes she does it when we say the word without sticking our tongue out, which has been fun to watch develop. She’s learning new words every day when in either of our arms, whether my wife is saying “happy happy happy!” when teaching her to smile or I’m listing off every single thing in our house in Bosnian so she knows words for everyday things.

In the latter case, teaching Bosnian full-time has been an interesting experience. There are words that I have forgotten over the years, which feels mildly embarrassing when I’m listing things off and draw a blank and quickly check Google Translate and suddenly recall it, but I think that’s the nature of living in a foreign land.

There are also words that have reappeared in my vocabulary; or maybe never left but hung out in the back of the classroom. When I’m talking to my daughter, I’ll speak in complete sentences and words (re)appear that I haven’t said in a very long time. The return of those words always surprises me, almost jolting me back, as if they busted open through the door that’s my mouth. They surprise my wife too, who is a great observer and says that she hasn’t heard that word before, which is notable considering we’ve been together for more than a decade and she’s heard me speak with my parents in Bosnian countless times.

My wife is learning new words too as they jump out of my mouth unexpectedly, as she’ll repeat words days later and confirm their meaning. She generally doesn’t speak Bosnian and only knows a handful of words but has always been able to piece together conversations through context clues to gain understanding.

Whatever comes of us all learning Bosnian, we hope to at least stick our tongues out at the same time as a family when someone says, “*jezik!*”.
