Our daughter, who is nearly six months old, started eating solids about two weeks ago, and it’s been going well thanks to her love of food and my wife’s research.

My wife is a fantastic researcher. She’s the type with an idea of what she wants and somehow finds it deep in Amazon, five-star reviews and all. This quality also makes her an extremely thorough and well-prepared mother, especially when it comes to prepping non-milk foods for our daughter.

As part of the prep, my wife identified foods that our daughter’s developing stomach can digest and then we visited the nearby farm stand for fresh fruits and vegetables. We returned with cucumbers, dill, honeydew, yellow-seeded watermelon, apples, peaches, pears, peas, and more, all of which our daughter ate as a puree mixed with my wife’s breast milk.

Our daughter loves to eat, asking for more and more spoonfuls, even grabbing the spoon herself and bringing it to her mouth, especially peas. We thought she loved farm-fresh applesauce and then she had peas, which made her ravenous. She ate the peas in record-time and had one of her longest stretches of sleep that night. Hopefully she will continue sleeping longer after solids and love peas when she becomes an adult (maybe I’m the one dreaming?).

The only obstacle that we faced was dairy. Our daughter does not like tart foods, like plain yogurt, sour cream, or eggs. We tried to gently give her all three of them mixed with breast milk, and she had a gag-like reaction to all of them, which was particularly painful for me as a dairy lover.

What’s more is that the dairy caused her to have something like acid reflux (and constipation) that led her to panicking, hyperventilating, and crying, which was admittedly scary. As a result, we decided not to offer her dairy anymore until we discuss it with the pediatrician at her next appointment.

As a parent, I never want to see my daughter struggle so even something as simple as that caused immense anxiety and stress. She was fine though and overall had a solid start to starting solids.
