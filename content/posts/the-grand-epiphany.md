I’m still waiting. I’m still waiting for The Grand Epiphany, the one where I transform into a changed man after becoming a father.

Everyone kept talking and writing about The Grand Epiphany. They said that I would change significantly, whether my personality or my ways. They said that I would no longer have time for my hobbies, my interests, my fun. Life was over.

It turns out my hobbies are kid-friendly. I like to eat, to talk, to walk, to write, to read. While our daughter can’t do all of those (yet), she’s already coming along for some of them, like walks. I strap her into the stroller and off we go for an hourlong walk, uphill, downhill, and flat across Main Street amongst people, some who have kids, others who do not.

When we’re not walking, I read baby books to her filled with animals and alphabets in two languages, and then we talk about how much we like reading and learning. She hasn’t learned to sound out words (yet), but she shares her thoughts through facial expressions, knowing that a smile spells h-a-p-p-y.

As for me, I’m still me. I look the same except a couple of new gray hairs on the sides of my head. I feel the same except I now have a softer heart, am more focused, and forget a lot. I still do the same things that I have always done except they are not on my time or place. For example, we order takeout now rather than dine-in.

In general, what used to be done on a whim requires skillful analysis of sleep patterns to find 30 minutes that can be used productively (one can hope!).

All of this is the same as it has always been, really. There may be a change in time, place, or method, but my life has largely remained the same with a baby. Maybe that says something about myself and my family life. Maybe I had The Grand Epiphany a long time ago. Maybe I’ve been mature all along.
