Three weeks ago, we bought an [activity center](https://www.target.com/p/skip-hop-explore-more-baby-s-view-3-stage-activity-center/-/A-51462297) for our daughter because she has built up her neck and overall body strength so quickly. She was also showing signs of boredom with her other toys, so we wanted a more engaging toy.

When we set it up and put our daughter in it for the first time, she neither knew how to sit in it nor use it. She would lean forward, lean backward, and [bring it around town](https://youtu.be/X77oEWnLN-w), smiling the entire time. She also blinked at the sound or movement of the toys anchored onto the activity center, and we wondered if it were too much mentally for her. But we kept putting her in the activity center because she seemed interested in it; at least judging by how long she stared at it when we held her.

Over the week after buying it, she started engaging with the toys, particularly the owl. The owl hides in a brown tree stump with only the top of its head visible. The tree has a green lever on to the side shaped like a leaf, and when it’s pushed down the owl rises out of stump. She kept slapping the top of the owl’s head in those first days, trying and failing to make it rise.

Then she saw the lever. She engaged with it too as the first week moved into the second, developing a feel for it. She kept touching it but not pushing it down, and I wanted to see if she would figure out on her own how it worked, and she did! She slammed her fist onto the lever one day, causing the owl to rise out of the tree, and she’s repeated it over and over, and we’ve been clapping for her, which is her favorite part of it all because the girl loves a standing ovation.

Now in her third week of using it, she’s still battling the owl. Knowing where it hides and how to make it come out, she’s navigating how to keep it out. She’s realized that holding down the lever means the owl stays out, but she’s trying to hold down the lever *and* grab the owl with the same hand, which is impossible.

She will figure it out, though. I can see her brain really working hard on it through her wide and focused eyes and her stubborn persistence, which has resulted in brain development over these last three weeks.
