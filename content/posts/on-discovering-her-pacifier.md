Our daughter has been “working” on soothing herself overnight. It usually means that she wakes up in the middle of the night in her crib, rolls onto her belly, and looks around, waiting for one of us to make eye contact as soon as possible.

Lately, we’ve been seeing what she does if we don’t come immediately. When she wakes, we open our eyes and listen closely to what she’s doing, which is usually tumbling or mumbling. Sometimes we turn onto our backs, raise our heads from our pillows, and watch her looking in our direction. Eventually she wins out and one of us picks her up (mostly my wife because she feeds her all the time).

One recent morning, my wife asked me if I had given our daughter her pacifier in the middle of the night. I was confused and shook my head. Then we looked at each other … our daughter must’ve found it on her own!

I imagine our daughter had woken up, rolled onto her belly, raised her head, locked her elbows out, and saw the pacifier in the opposite corner of the crib where I’ve been placing it every night since we came home from the hospital, and dragged her belly full of milk across the crib, finding comfort in it all on her own. Considering we put her to sleep near the footboard, it was a long, impressive crawl.

These strides all feel like steps toward her taking to her own feet.
