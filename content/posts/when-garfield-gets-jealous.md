One of the unexpected challenges with raising our daughter has been our cat. He’s an orange Tabby, and he believes that no one else should receive attention, including our daughter.

When we brought our daughter home, we had to shut him out of our bedroom, which caused him to meow at our door for a week. It was heartbreaking but necessary for the safety of our daughter.

After the week, he adjusted to sleeping on his own in the room across ours because we made two changes to accommodate him:

1.  We let him come into our room and hang out as long as we were awake while our daughter slept.
2.  We tucked him in every night by carrying him from our room to the other room and petting him and telling him good night.

These two changes worked well at night, but another problem presented itself when our daughter napped during the day: He didn’t like her napping in my arms. That was his place to nap; at least it had been before our daughter was born.

In the four years leading up to our daughter’s birth, my wife and I treated our cat like he was the “Prince of Dubai,” as my wife says—our biggest mistake. He had the entire house and us to himself at all times, as we let him sleep on us, sleep between us, cuddle, etc. It created codependency for him that was continually enabled by us unknowingly; we simply showed him love because we loved him (and still do).

But when our daughter was born, the codependency became a bigger problem because he deliberately meowed when our daughter was sleeping in my arms. He did it every day as soon as he saw her asleep, causing her to wake up and/or have interrupted sleep.

After four months (we waited too long), we needed to take action and decided to take him to the vet, who prescribed 10MG of Prozac daily. Now he’s calm and okay with our daughter being part of our family.

But I still wonder if he’s always had this anxiety, which contributed to the codependency, and how many mistakes we made along the way out of seemingly harmless love. That’s the tough part about having pets: You don’t know what mistakes you’re making until they manifest in their character traits and actions years later.

It’s tough about having kids too, or so I’ve heard and will find out.
