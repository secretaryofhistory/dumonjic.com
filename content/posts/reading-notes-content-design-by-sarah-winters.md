## Discovery

-   Goal is to understand:
    -   Audience’s view
    -   Experts’ view
    -   Organization’s business goals
    -   Missing information based on experts’ anecdotes, such as user behaviors and repeated patterns or instructions
    -   What the next steps should be
-   How to run discovery:
    -   Get product experts, developers, designers, delivery managers, and potentially audience members on a call
    -   Introduce a concept at a high level and ask how participants experience it
    -   Ask open ended questions
    -   Ask audience to create sketches
    -   Create a visual mind map or representation that’s visible to everyone 
    -   Take notes

## Research

-   Goal is to understand:
    -   Who your audience really is
    -   What they want to know
    -   How they feel about it
    -   How to speak to them
    -   What vocabulary they use
    -   Their mental models
-   Prioritize the audience who:
    -   Knows the discipline and/or product and have negative emotions toward it
    -   Doesn’t know the discipline and/or product and want to understand
    -   Knows the discipline and/or product and have positive emotions 
-   Start with search data and look for patterns/duplication:
    -   Internal search data:
        -   How customers use search functionality in product or on website, specifically what words they use to describe product
        -   How many people visit a page
        -   Where they came from (referrals)
        -   How quickly they leave (bounce rate)
        -   How much time they spend on page
    -   External search data:
        -   Google Trends
        -   Google Analytics
        -   Social media and forums, like Reddit

### User Stories and Job Stories

-   User story:
    -   As a \[person in a particular role\], I want to \[perform an action or find something\], so that \[I can achieve my goal\]
-   Job story:
    -   When \[there’s a particular situation\], I want to \[perform an action or find something\], so I can \[achieve my goal\]
-   Job stories are better for less audiences or one audience
-   Job stories can be more reliable than user stories because behaviors change a lot. Another way to put it is jobs change less than behaviors
-   Set an acceptance criteria for the stories by identifying exactly what the person needs to feel accomplished 

### Journey Mapping

-   Journey map is a timeline of steps or decisions that the audience completes in your product to achieve a goal 
-   Every decision is a potential content point 
-   Formulate maps in the following way:
    -   Prompt
    -   Finding/discovery
    -   Act on findings
    -   Achieving
-   Journey maps start with a prompt, which is a thought, feeling, or perception that leads your audience to your product
    -   For example, an IT administrator hears his teachers are frustrated with policies blocking websites used in classrooms and wants to see if he can allow them to override some policy rules
-   Map all of the thoughts and decision points a user needs to make
-   Aim for 5 prompt points based on working assumptions that arise from prior knowledge and then move into research to validate the assumptions

### Mental Models

-   Mental models tell us what people think about your product, including their preconceptions about it and what they believe to be true and false regardless of facts
-   Content design should always do one of the following 3 Rs:
    -   Reflect a confirmed mental model
    -   Rebut a mental model
    -   Re-educate the audience 
-   Identify opportunities to cross sell when addressing the 3 Rs

### Empathy Mapping

-   Four quadrants on a map:
    -   Say
    -   Think
    -   Feel
    -   Do
-   Complete the map based on user research for every step of the journey

## Design

-   Prioritize what research shows users want, information that improve organizational reputation, and then things that the most developer time
-   Convert job stories into content, such as:
    -   Story: An IT administrator hears his teachers are frustrated with policies blocking websites used in classrooms and wants to see if he can allow them to override some policy rules
    -   Article: Explains how Teacher Override
-   Determine how much content you want to publish and how much control you want over adjacent information, such as third-party dependencies that need explanation too

### Sketching

-   3-minute session works well
-   Take a piece of paper and ask what the content could look like
-   Use boxes for videos, blocks for images, etc
-   Add headings as signposts but not paragraphs

### Miscellaneous Content Notes 

-   Front-load headings with most important information
-   5 seconds to get someone’s attention
-   Subheadings help the audience know what information they will get when they skim the page
-   First paragraph should always orient the reader in 3 seconds
-   80/20 rule:
    -   Put first the information that 80% of your audience is looking for
    -   Make the remaining 20% discoverable by search 
-   Before you publish any content, identify:
    -   What success looks like for the content
    -   How long it could be around
    -   When it may need to be updated
