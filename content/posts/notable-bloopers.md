As first-time parents, fascinating moments have happened because we’ve been overtired, sleepless, hungry, dehydrated, or all at the same time. These moments wouldn’t have happened if we weren’t parents, I think, which makes them fun. Here are four brief stories of our parental bloopers.

---

After my wife birthed our daughter, she was a tired superhero. Her mind and body were so exhausted that she didn’t know where she was on one of the first nights we came home.

In the middle of one of those first nights, she heard our daughter crying and simply said, “Yes,” aloud.

When I asked her to whom she was talking, she said the nurse.

I sat confused.

She explained that she was answering “Yes” to the nurse who had offered her to take our daughter for a few hours so we could get sleep, like she had done on one of the first days after birth.

I explained that we’re at home.

The next day, the nurse from the hospital called and asked how the night went. My wife, still exhausted, said that we were “delusional.”

She meant *delirious*.

---

As part of our new morning routine, I make us coffee, bring it upstairs for us to drink in bed, and we watch Main Street wake up.

My coffee takes a little longer to brew because it’s a Bialetti on the stovetop, so I spend the wait time preparing things around the house, including replacing the old K-cup and water in my wife’s Keurig. When my coffee is finishing, I push the start button for my wife’s coffee and in 10 seconds the brouhaha is over and all is brewed.

One morning, we took our coffee (and baby) downstairs after a week of drinking it upstairs, and my wife noticed that she was running out of K-cups.

“How’s that possible?” she said.

I shrugged.

“When you make the coffee in the morning, do you just push the button?”

I nodded, slowly. Then I said:

“Yeah, I toss the old one … “

“That’s not old. I put in a new one every night.”

Apparently, my wife prepares her morning coffee *the night before* by putting in a new pod in her Keurig, so she or I can simply push the button the next morning to start brewing.

Who knew?

---

In the first few weeks, we took *a lot* of pictures with our daughter, and I noticed that the pictures mostly consisted of me holding our daughter. There were very few of my wife.

After thinking it over, I realized that she didn’t have many photos with our daughter because she was always breastfeeding her, so I made a mental note to take photos when the opportunities presented themselves.

Unfortunately, I was mental in those early days and decided one night that the best time to take a photo was at like 2 AM while my wife was feeding our daughter. I grabbed my phone from the nightstand, opened the Camera app blurry-eyed, and took a photo of my wife feeding our daughter. In the act, I forgot that the sound was on so the phone emitted a camera-shutter sound in a silent room.

My wife burst out in laughter. I did too. We laughed pretty damn hard, partly because we were so tired. The photo is no longer around, but the memory lives on.

---

During diaper changes, there’s a waiting period that happens to prevent rashes from forming on a baby’s butt. That period takes place after wiping the butt clean and before putting on a new diaper, leaving you sitting around for a minute or two until the baby’s butt is dry. Naturally, I try to entertain our daughter during the time, making her smile and laugh by saying nonsensical things.

This one time, we were waiting and I decided to open the clean diaper and put it on our daughter’s head so it sat like this, “^”. Then I introduced her as a “Founding Farter” and went on and on about her accolades, eliciting laughs from her, when she suddenly peed everywhere.

And I mean *everywhere*. She peed on herself, on me, on my pillow, and our sheets, so everything had to be changed. I had thought it was going to be a quick diaper change (Nope!) and skipped placing her on the changing table.

Oh well. At least she laughed.
