Our daughter discovered her hands. She always knew that she had them, I think, but she didn’t know that they could be useful to her.

It happened a week or two ago, when she started sticking her fingers in her mouth to soothe herself after waking up. It happened suddenly like so many things do with babies, so my wife and I were surprised when we heard her across the room. She now sticks a finger or two in her chubby cheek, which always reminds me of the [close-ups of squirrels](https://static.vecteezy.com/system/resources/previews/000/716/297/non_2x/golden-mantled-squirrel-with-cheeks-full-of-food-photo.jpg) with acorns stored in their cheeks, and sometimes even when she’s drinking milk from the bottle; it’s as challenging as it sounds—for her and us.

But her hands weren’t always this familiar and useful to her. They previously were kind of floating expressions of freedom and relief from tension. She used to stretch her fingers far out, like party blowers, to relieve the tension in her body or sometimes scrape the skin off of her face (figuratively speaking), including her eyeballs. It never seemed like she was truly in control or expressing herself; rather her limbic system was expressing itself independently of her mind and emotions.

It’s made me wonder how much or how long we have control over our bodies. I’ve wondered this for a long time, particularly when I started running after the COVID pandemic and found myself awake at 6 a.m., planning a slow wake-up with a coffee and finding my legs literally getting off of the couch on their own as if they were starting a run and my upper body lurching forward. Perhaps it was muscle memory, as I was running nearly every day at the time, but it has always made me wonder if we are in charge or something else.

It seems that at least our daughter is now in charge at times. She’s not only using her hands to soothe herself but to smack her arms and legs against her mattress and to pick up and fling things, whether it’s a ball, a book, or another toy. It’s fascinating watching her develop these motor skills; or maybe it’s her limbic system developing them and she’s watching too.
