Earlier this week, I took our daughter on an evening stroller ride toward Main Street. She took longer than usual to fall asleep during the ride (rush hour), so when the ride’s planned end came, I pushed the stroller past our place into another complex.

In the complex, a little boy was sitting next to his mom in their parking space and drawing with colored chalk. As I walked past them, the little boy stared and stared at me. Suddenly I heard:

“Mom, why does that man have a baby?”

I chuckled, chalking it up to the kid’s curiosity and humor. But as I walked away, I wondered if it meant more than a simple question. I thought about the famous six-word story (mis)attributed to Ernest Hemingway, which always made me think of the worst case scenario:

> For sale: Baby shoes, never worn.

That story appeared in my mind during the walk because I didn’t see the kid’s dad outside; it’s possible that he was simply at work, but what if he were out of his life?

I reminded myself that parents can choose to have kids, but kids can’t choose their parents, so it’s imperative that parents serve as good role models, whether they are parents by blood or passersby pushing their daughters in strollers.

Being a good role model is a personal challenge. I’ve never been a bad role model but never have I cared to be a good one. I’ve always been sharp-tongued, honest beyond belief, and willing to bend rules, whether they are local laws or cultural norms. I can’t imagine that changing (even if I soften); if anything, I can already see our daughter becoming similar.

A couple of days after our stroller ride, we went out for another. I went down to the same complex, making a loop, when I saw the same kid standing by his bike and seemingly his little sister. They stared and then pointed at the man with a baby, whispering something.
