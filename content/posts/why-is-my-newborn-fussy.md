One thing no one prepares you for in parenthood is taking care of someone who can’t communicate their needs.

Before our daughter was born, I interacted with people who did not speak the same language for example, but those exchanges were inconsequential and brief; and didn’t end in cries. I generally interacted with people who could express their needs through speaking or writing. It made life easier because I either clearly knew what they needed or could ask follow-up questions to clarify.

With our seven-week-old daughter who obviously can’t speak or write yet, she cries and I wonder what she needs. This is significantly easier now than it was in the first week, but the early experience remains in my mind.

Most of our daughter’s needs start with an audible sound. It may be a groan, grunt, or cry. Each may mean something different.

When she groans, she’s usually waking or soothing herself mid-sleep; and in some cases shes suffering from gas pains that lead to cries. In the case of sleeping, she groans and raises her legs in the air and swings them side to side like a pendulum, which she eventually stops and remains asleep. Either result from a groan is a relatively calm result.

But when she’s grunting, she’s almost always soiling her diaper. The trouble with this is it can go on for quite some time and attempts to soothe her with pacifier are futile. It seems that she grunts through her nose and her mouth, causing the pacifier to fall out. Sometimes she simply doesn’t want the pacifier. It always surprises me.

The trickiest of them is the cry because it could truly mean any of the below, which requires ruling them out one by one while she screams:

-   Soiled diaper
-   Gas pains
-   Hungry
-   Overtired
-   Infested with boogers
-   Needs contact
-   Something unexpected

The interesting thing about her cries is that they’re not all the same. Some are long, gasping cries that cause tears to stream down her chubby cheeks (and even into her ears!). Others are (dare I say) false cries, an *ooo-wahh* or *wehh*, that signal attention is needed. But attention for what? Guess!

All of this requires troubleshooting, which becomes easier as the weeks pass because this little person entity only needs so much at such a young age. In some ways, that’s what makes it easy too.
