Yesterday morning, I was entertaining my six-month-old daughter when she had a surprisingly emotionally intelligent reaction to an external factor.

I was playing Balkan music because it’s another way for her to learn the Bosnian language instead of only me (or in addition to) reading her books, and one of our commonly played songs came on called *Tugo Nesrećo* by Crvena Jabuka. It’s admittedly a melancholy song, though a valuable listen because of the clear pronunciation of lyrics, which helps our daughter develop her vocabulary.

In the song’s refrain, the lead singer repeats the song title, with the “u” in the first word (“*Tugo*”) dragged out (“*Tuuuuu-go*”). To demonstrate the dragged out letter, I lowered my left hand with my palm facing downward and raised it at the same pace as the singer’s voice, like a conductor would.

At first, our daughter observed what I was doing with interest, so I repeated the hand motion each time the refrain was sung, syncing the motioning to the singing, which caused me to look away from my daughter’s face.

The next time I looked at her, her eyebrow and eyelids were pink, and her upper lip was tucked into her lower, showing a sad face. She was on the verge of bursting into tears.

I put my left hand down and stopped the song.

I looked at her again, and her facial expression eased. Her lips rested. Her eyelids and eyebrows returned to their normal milk color, matching the rest of her skin tone. She was relieved.

I realized that she may have picked up on the sad tone of the song through my hand motions and the periodic glances at my phone that played the song, and [emotional intelligence may develop in children sooner](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822\(11\)00654-3?_returnURL=https%3A%2F%2Flinkinghub.elsevier.com%2Fretrieve%2Fpii%2FS0960982211006543%3Fshowall%3Dtrue) than I thought.
